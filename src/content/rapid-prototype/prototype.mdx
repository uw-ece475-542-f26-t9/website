The wearable will consist of two major parts: the main housing for the microcontroller,
battery, and on-board circuit, and the MyoWare sensor. The housing will be a 3D-printed
case on the wrist, secured tightly with an elastic strap to prevent the IMU from moving
separately from the arm. It will contain the battery and the perfboard circuit, most
likely stacked atop one another. More details on the perfboard circuit follow in the
Hardware section.

The second component is the MyoWare 2.0 sensor and its electrodes. The sensor itself will
sit on the bicep, with the two differential electrodes resting at their default spacing of
about 2.5 cm. Keeping these main electrodes directly attached to the MyoWare avoids a long
cable run for the weak EMG signal, which is the most likely place for excessive noise to
be introduced. The reference electrode will sit on a bony part of the elbow, attached with
a short lead. While the leads (in this case none since the are directly attached) to
the two differential electrodes must be of equal length,
the reference lead may differ without issue. The MyoWare will be connected to the main
device via a flexible wire running up the arm and across the elbow. It will be taped in a
couple of places to keep it from shifting, but left with some slack around the joint for
user comfort and to prevent pulling on the wire with each arm movement.

## Hardware

<BlockDiagram />

The main system consists of a XIAO ESP32-S3, an MPU6050 IMU, a DRV2605L driving a Linear
Resonant Actuator (LRA) for haptic feedback, and a small voltage-sensing circuit. These are
soldered onto a 4 × 6 cm perfboard, while the 500 mAh LiPo, the LRA, and the MyoWare sit
off-board and plug in over JST-PH connectors. Power runs from the LiPo through a small
switch board, and the switched battery rail (3.0–4.2 V) feeds the XIAO's battery pads, the
DRV2605L, the battery sense divider, and a dedicated 3.0 V LDO for the MyoWare. The
DRV2605L has 47 µF and 10 µF capacitors at its supply to absorb the current spikes when
the motor fires. Giving the MyoWare its own regulator keeps the load spikes from the
ESP32's BLE radio and processing out of the EMG supply, where they would show up as noise.
This leaves the XIAO's 3.3 V output powering only the IMU. The XIAO's USB-C port handles
both programming and charging, with the LiPo charging at around 100 mA.

The IMU and DRV2605L share one I²C bus on pins D4 (SDA) and D5 (SCL), at addresses 0x68
and 0x5A. The DRV2605L drives the LRA over a 2-pin cable, so the motor can sit against the
skin outside the enclosure. The MyoWare connects with a 3-pin cable carrying 3.0 V, ground,
and its RAW output, which the XIAO reads on analog pin A0. Its ground runs on a separate
wire straight to a XIAO GND pin rather than sharing a path with the rest of the board,
which keeps return current from the other parts from shifting the reference the EMG signal
is measured against. Finally, the battery sense circuit is a 100 kΩ / 100 kΩ divider with
a 0.1 µF capacitor to steady the reading. It halves the battery voltage to fit within the
ADC's range and is read on A1 to track the remaining charge.

The software stack will consist primarily of the companion app, "Fatigue Coach," built in
Flutter/Dart with Material 3 theming. It targets Android and iOS, but also runs on web and
desktop platforms. The app connects to the wearable over BLE and only lists devices that
advertise the firmware's custom service ID, so other nearby Bluetooth devices are hidden.
Once connected, it receives three data streams: motion-sensor readings at 50 Hz,
muscle-signal (EMG) summaries at 10 Hz, and rep events that carry the rep count and each
rep's range of motion. These arrive in a compact binary format, which a small protocol
layer decodes. The app's state lives in two controller classes built on Flutter's own
`ChangeNotifier`, with no third-party state library. One manages the connection and
passes the sensor data on to the rest of the app; the other runs workout sets. A set's rep
count is the change in the wearable's running counter since the set began, and a set ends
automatically if the connection drops. The app includes an animated splash screen, a
connect page with a live sensor check, and Home, Workout, and History tabs, with charts
for arm motion, reps per set, and weekly activity. All data stays on the phone in
`shared_preferences` (a simple key-value store): each finished set is saved as a small
JSON record with start time, duration, reps, and an optional peak-fatigue value. A
database and account system for tracking user data across devices is a stretch goal if
development time allows.
